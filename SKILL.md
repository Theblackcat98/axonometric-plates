# Axonometric Plates

A plate is an architectural drawing that runs in the browser: one self-contained HTML file, a building built procedurally in Three.js and presented as an axonometric on drafting paper — flat ink-and-wash surfaces, one light direction, the structure opened up rather than hidden, free to orbit.

The existing plates in this repo are the specification by example: `plates/chichen-itza.html`, `plates/christ-the-redeemer.html`, `plates/colosseum.html`, `plates/great-wall.html`, `plates/hanging-gardens.html`, `plates/hanging-gardens-reconstruction.html`, `plates/hoogbouw.html`, `plates/machu-picchu.html`, `plates/petra.html`, `plates/pisa.html`, `plates/taj-mahal.html`. Before writing a new one, read `plates/template.html` (the canonical engine with a demo subject) and study whichever existing plate is closest to the new subject.

## The contract

These are not defaults to tune per plate — they are what makes the plates a series.

**File shape**
- One HTML file, no build step, no assets. Two CDN scripts only: Three.js r128 (`cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/three.js/r128/three.min.js`) and OrbitControls (`cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.128.0/examples/js/controls/OrbitControls.js`), then one inline `<script>`.
- Page background `#e3ded4`; renderer clear colour `0xe5e0d6`; `pixelRatio` capped at `Math.min(window.devicePixelRatio, 2)`.
- Optional bottom-left `#hint` caption (`<SUBJECT> · <LOCATION/DATE> · AXONOMETRIC STUDY   /   DRAG TO ORBIT · DOUBLE-CLICK TO RESET`), sitting on its own opaque paper patch so site grid and context lines never bleed through the type:
  `position: absolute; left: 18px; bottom: 14px; z-index: 2; background: #ede9e0; padding: 3px 6px; max-width: calc(100% - 48px); color: rgba(65,53,43,0.68); font: 11px/1.4 ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, monospace; letter-spacing: 0.08em; text-transform: uppercase; pointer-events: none;`
  (`max-width: calc(100% - 48px)` so it wraps cleanly on 390px phones).

**Drafting paper** — a 2D canvas overlay above the WebGL canvas: fill `#ede9e0`, orthogonal grid every 28px at `rgba(130,120,108,0.09)`, per-pixel grain ±9 from its own `mulberry32(8841)`, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, `opacity: 0.55`, `pointer-events: none`. Regenerated on resize.

**Camera** — `THREE.OrthographicCamera`, near −200 / far 300 (widen to −400 / 900 for deep mountain or precinct scenes), positioned on the true axonometric diagonal `(tx + d, ty + 0.78·d, tz + d)` looking at `targetCenter = new THREE.Vector3(tx, ty, tz)`. Use `(0, targetY, 0)` (with `targetY` at roughly half the total building height) for symmetric subjects, or an explicit 3D `targetCenter` when forecourts, ridges, or leaning shafts shift the visual weight off-origin (`taj-mahal`, `machu-picchu`, `pisa`). Damped OrbitControls (`dampingFactor 0.08`); double-click restores `defaultCamPos`, `targetCenter`, and `zoom = 1`.
- **Axonometric orientation**: from the default camera at `(+d, +y, +d)` with upper-left sun `(-0.35, 0.25, 0.90)`, vertical lines (`+Y`) are strictly vertical on screen, the **`+Z` face** points toward **bottom-left** (sunlit primary facade), the **`+X` face** points toward **bottom-right** (shaded secondary facade), `-X` points top-left, `-Z` points top-right, `(+1, 0, -1)` runs purely horizontally to **screen-right**, and `(+1, 0, +1)` points toward the camera (so cylinder silhouette checks test where a surface normal is perpendicular to `(0.707, 0, 0.707)`, i.e. `Math.abs(nx * 0.707 - nz * 0.707) > 0.96`).
- **Aspect-aware framing**: start with `frustumSize ≈ 1.22–1.4×` the subject's total height so the monument fills ~70–85% of viewport height, and check portrait phone framing (390×844) separately — a single `frustumSize` that works at 16:9 will clip wide subjects horizontally at 9:19.5. For wide subjects (`colosseum`, `hanging-gardens`), replace the fixed scalar with `fittedFrustum(aspect) = Math.max(NEED_H, NEED_W / aspect)` in both init and `resize`.
- **Polar clamp**: when the model sits on a closed ground slab and is not meant to be viewed from underneath, clamp `controls.minPolarAngle = Math.PI * 0.10` and `controls.maxPolarAngle = Math.PI * 0.472`.

**Determinism** — all randomness flows through one `mulberry32` PRNG seeded with a year that matters to the subject (1931 the statue was dedicated, AD 80 the Colosseum, 1173 Pisa's first stone, 312 BC Petra as Nabataean capital, 1450 Machu Picchu, 1632 Taj Mahal). Never `Math.random()`. A plate renders identically on every load.

**Wash** — surfaces are raw vertex-coloured triangles, no lights, no materials with shading. Each material is a palette of five steps `{lit, mid, shade, top, bot}` — diluted marker/watercolour tones on warm stone paper, never saturated RGB. Face colour is chosen by `computeDirectionalHex` against a fixed light direction `(-0.35, 0.25, 0.90)`: `ny > 0.65` → top, `ny < −0.65` → bot, else dot product `> 0.28` → lit, `> −0.18` → mid, else shade. Per-face lightness jitter of ±0.009–0.018 keeps large walls from going flat. The wash mesh uses `MeshBasicMaterial({vertexColors: true, polygonOffset: true, polygonOffsetFactor: 1.4, polygonOffsetUnits: 1.4})`: because `polygonOffset` pushes the solid faces slightly back in the depth buffer, the wash mesh acts as an **automatic 3D hidden-line removal buffer** for all `LineSegments` behind it.
- **Back-face culling & winding**: `template.html` leaves `MeshBasicMaterial` single-sided, so any quad with flipped winding vanishes as a missing face — which not only drops the colour wash, but lets background ink lines bleed through the building like a wireframe. Either set `side: THREE.DoubleSide` on `washMat` (essential whenever cutaways, thin parapets, lofted rings, or mirrored `-X` limbs are visible from both sides, as in `taj-mahal`, `christ-the-redeemer`, `petra`, `colosseum`, `hanging-gardens`) or auto-orient quads against their outward normal before emitting (`pushQuadN` in `chichen-itza`, swapping `(p1, p3)` when `(p1−p0)×(p2−p0) · n < 0`) and `.reverse()` profile rings when mirroring across an axis.
- **Coplanar z-fighting discipline**: `polygonOffset` only separates *ink* from *wash*; two wash quads on the same plane will shimmer. Never place a wash decal, dark doorway void, spandrel panel, jali screen, bandcourse, water surface, or cast-shadow quad at `0` offset on a parent face — either cut an actual opening in the parent geometry (`arcadeWall`, sunken basin/hypogeum cutout in the ground slab) or step the detail out/in by `0.015–0.04` world units along the face normal. Surface ink lines sit `0.004–0.015` proud of the wash face they belong to.

**Ink** — WebGL locks `LineBasicMaterial` to `1px`, so line weight hierarchy is achieved by accumulating `LineSegments` into six standard layers (plus an optional seventh `foam` layer for active white water) with distinct colours, opacities, and multi-stroke offsets:

| layer | hex | opacity | carries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bold | `0x211b17` | 0.92 | Scale 1 (structural): primary silhouettes, arch intrados, columns, roof ridges (triple-stroked with micro-offsets to read as a 0.8mm nib) |
| medium | `0x332a24` | 0.80 | Scale 2 (architectural): secondary edges, window mullions/transoms, capitals, balustrade posts, step risers, crease lines |
| fine | `0x4a3f37` | 0.44 | Scale 3 (textural): parallel shadow hatching, stone joints, roof tile seams, paving grids |
| rust | `0x8c4531` | 0.64 | Scale 3 (textural): terracotta/brick course hatching, cornice soldier bricks, arch extrados hoods, section-cut hatching |
| teal | `0x527d80` | 0.52 | Scale 3 (textural): vertical window glass reflections, intarsia, water ripples, glazed tile bands, foliage accents |
| faint | `0x6b6157` | 0.32 | Context: site street grid, ghost neighbours, backdrop peaks, sky construction rays |
| foam *(opt.)* | `0xf4fbf8` | 0.88 | Active water: waterfall crests, falling streaks, splash fans, bright basin ripples (`hanging-gardens`) |

`addLineWorld(p1, p2, layer, overshoot)` extends each segment slightly past its endpoints (default 0.022) for a technical-pen corner overshoot and jitters the ends by ±0.007 from the PRNG (faint layer: no jitter). Bold is rationed — if everything is bold, nothing is.

**Script structure** — numbered sections, in order: (1) PRNG + paper overlay, (2) scene + camera + controls + reset, (3) palette + wash/ink engine, (4) subject transform if any, (5+) builders, then subject assembly, site furniture, and finally geometry materialisation + render loop. `plates/template.html` is this skeleton with the engine already in place.

## Builders

Work in points-as-arrays `[x, y, z]`, in world units where the subject's real proportions set the scale.

- `pushQuadWorld / pushTriWorld(p…, hex, shadeShift)` — the only way wash enters the scene. Pair with a normal-driven helper (`quadN` or `pushQuadN`) so face shading is locked to world normals regardless of camera orbit.
- `addLineWorld(p1, p2, layer, overshoot)` and `addDashedLineWorld(p1, p2, dashLen, gapLen, layer)` — the only way ink enters the scene.
- `subjectPoint(x, y, z)` — every subject builder emits through this hook. Identity for an upright subject; Pisa overrides it with a curved leaning-spine table, and Hoogbouw with a sinusoidal bow + twist, so the whole structure warps without any builder knowing. For tilted sub-assemblies on an otherwise upright subject, add a local transform hook alongside `subjectPoint` — e.g. `headPoint(x, y, z)` pitching Christ's head 14.5° forward around the neck pivot, or `tiltX / tiltZ` leaning the Taj Mahal's minarets 1.4° outward from the terrace.
- `addCylinder(y0, y1, rOuter, rInner, segs, pal, opts)` — drums and rings: optional `courses` (rust layer when the palette is brick/terracotta, fine otherwise), directional shade hatching on the unlit right flank only (`lightDot < −0.12`, 2–3 vertical `fine` strokes per shaded segment), automatic silhouette bolding (`Math.abs(nx * 0.707 - nz * 0.707) > 0.96`), hollow form with inner wall + annulus caps when `rInner > 0`.
- `addCircularCornice(y, h, rBase, segs, opts)` & rectilinear `addCorniceBand(x, y, z, w, h, d, overhang)` — three stacked moldings separating floor/tier strata: lower astragal (`~22%` height, `0.4×` overhang in `cream`), middle frieze (`~52%` height in `terracotta`/`brickDark` with horizontal `courses` and dense vertical `rust` soldier-brick hatching), and upper overhanging corona (`~26%` height, full overhang in `cream` with `bold` edges and dentil ticks underneath).
- `addColumn(angle, radius, y0, y1, colRadius, opts)` & `addCircularArcadeArches(ySpring, yTop, rOuter, rInner, numCols, opts)` — radial colonnades (`pisa.html`): place free-standing columns (square plinth, octagonal fluted shaft, flared abacus capital), then span each bay `(a0, a1)` with 6–8 cosine-spaced voussoir steps (`phi` from `0` to `π`). Emit the outer spandrel quad up to `yTop`, the deep-shadowed curved arch soffit (`dark.bot`) between `rOuter` and `rInner`, `bold` intrados lines, `rust` extrados hood (`+0.24`), and radial voussoir joints, backed by a darker `coreWall` inner cylinder (`rCore < rOuter`).
- `addWorldBox(x, y, z, w, h, d, pal, layer)` & annotated `addBox(x, y, z, w, h, d, pal, opts)` — axis-aligned boxes with directional face shading, per-module `shadeShift`, and optional `opts.courses` (horizontal `rust`/`fine` lines offset `+0.01` outside `+Z`/`+X`) and `opts.vHatchZ` / `opts.vHatchX` vertical hatching (`hoogbouw.html`).
- **Rectilinear facade kit** (`addWindowPanel`, `addCantedBayWindow`, `addOpenFrame` in `hoogbouw.html`):
  - *Recessed multipane windows* (`addWindowPanel`): recess the `teal`/`tealDeep` glass plane `0.12` units inside the wall, frame with a projecting `terracotta` sill and `cream` lintel, divide into casements (`bold` outer frame, `medium` mullions and transom bar at `74%` height, `fine` muntins), and draw **two vertical offset strokes in `teal` ink inside each pane** to read as glass reflection.
  - *Protruding canted bay windows* (`addCantedBayWindow`): 3-faceted trapezoidal prism jutting out from the wall — sculpted `terracotta` corbel apron at the base (`rust` hatching), multipane `teal` glass across left chamfer / front / right chamfer, and overhanging hood moulding.
  - *Open post-and-beam frames* (`addOpenFrame`): perimeter grid of slender square piers (`courses: 7`) around an empty interior volume for loggias, stilted sky-bridges, and breezeways.
- `addArchBay(xL, zL, xR, zR, y0, y1, depth, opts)` & `arcadeWall(faceFrame, y0, y1, opts)` — framed arch openings along a planar wall (`taj-mahal`'s `addMughalArchBay`, `hanging-gardens`'s `arcadeWall`, `petra`'s `facePoint` panels): solid piers at both ends, spandrel strips rising from the arch curve `yAt(t)` to the architrave, a recessed reveal/soffit and deep niche back wall (`voidd`/`dark`), an optional screen/jali inset or glazed spandrel band, and a parapet above. Arches are built as real recessed openings through `pushQuadWorld`/`addLineWorld` — never as a flat dark rectangle with an arch outline drawn on top.
- `addBulbousDome(cx, cz, yBase, domeH, rBase, segs, opts)` — an onion/bulbous dome as a profile table of `[heightFraction, radiusFraction, palette]` rows lofted around the axis: swelling past the drum radius, an inverted lotus collar in terracotta at the neck, a finial on top. Latitudes get line rings only at profile breaks — a dome ruled like a cage reads as glass, not marble.
- `addChhatri(cx, cz, y0, radius, totalH, opts)` — the kiosk: a small drum (octagonal reads best at plate scale), columns or a cornice ring, and a miniature dome cap. Corner punctuation on roofs and minarets.
- `addLoftedRing(ringA, ringB, pal, opts)` + `computeQuadNormal` — connects two N-point polygonal cross-sections (`christ-the-redeemer`, `petra`). Computes each facet's outward normal for palette selection, draws longitudinal crease lines where adjacent facet normals diverge (`dot < 0.88` → `medium`, view-silhouette sign flip `vPrev * vCurr <= 0` → `bold`), interpolates horizontal masonry courses (`lerpPt`), hatches shaded facets, and optionally caps the top/bottom rings with a polygon fan. Use this for sculpted robes, torsos, octagonal pedestals, battered pylons, urns, and scale figures.
- **Oriented path & ridge frames** (`framePoint`, `addOrientedBox`, `addTaperedBlock`, `addHipRoof`, `nodeFrames` in `great-wall` and `machu-picchu`) — when a subject follows a meandering ridge or rotated urban grid, define local frames `{x, z, y, ux, uz, vx, vz}` (tangent `u`, outward normal `v`). Emit battered walls (`hu0, hv0` at base → `hu1, hv1` at crest), mitered curtain walls lofted between station frames, grade-adaptive walkways (smooth ramp quads on gentle grades, discrete stepped treads when `slope > threshold`), and gabled/hipped roofs in local `(u, v, y)` coordinates.
- **Elliptical & radial plan primitives** (`EP`, `EN`, `angIn`, `surfQuad`, `annQuad`, `radQuad`, `addSectionCut` in `colosseum`) — parameterise elliptical monuments by `(rx, rz, a)` with analytical outward normal `(cos a / rx, 0, sin a / rz)` normalised. When cutting open tiered arcades or ruin breaks, snap angular cut ranges (`INTACT_A`, `INTACT_B`) to exact bay multiples (`bay * 2π / N`) so every section break lands cleanly on a solid radial pier rather than mid-arch, and close the exposed break with a hatched `addSectionCut` face (`cut` palette + diagonal `rust`/`fine` cross-hatching).
- **Terrain, cliffs, and backdrop massifs** (`great-wall`, `machu-picchu`, `petra`):
  - *Swept ridge terrain*: loft a cross-section profile (`[lateralOffset, drop]` pairs) along spine stations, shade facets by normal and elevation band, and clamp the outer skirt quads at the ground slab `GY` so mountain flanks never punch below the plinth.
  - *Craggy backdrop peaks* (`addPeak`, `addShoulderRidge` in `machu-picchu`): loft multi-ring massifs from a stepped/shouldered profile table (never a smooth cone) with off-vertical centre drift and seeded per-vertex radius jitter (`±15%`). Colour exclusively in the palest `peak`/`ghost` wash and `faint` ink so a towering backdrop peak frames the monument without competing for ink weight.
  - *Stratified cliffs* (`petra`): stack horizontal rock strata with varying `inset` and `front` depths, horizontal bedding lines, and vertical weathering fissures kept strictly outside the carved facade zone.
- **Water, foliage, shadows, and scale cues**:
  - *Pools & cascades* (`hanging-gardens`, `hanging-gardens-reconstruction`, `taj-mahal`): build pools (`basin`) with a raised stone rim, recessed `water` quad, and concentric `teal`/`foam` ripple lines; build waterfalls (`cascade`) as closed thin boxes with vertical `water`/`waterDk` bands, an overhanging spill lip, `foam` crest and falling streaks, splash fan, and mist triangles. Where a pool, catino, or hypogeum drops below the ground plane, cut an opening in the ground slab (`addGroundWithOpening`) instead of running the ground box through it.
  - *Planting & cast shadows*: distinguish between **drafting-margin shrubs** (`addSketchShrub` scattered on the outer survey grid: a vertical stem, diagonal shadow tick, and 4-segment diamond crown in pure `medium`/`fine` ink with *no* green fill, as in `pisa` and `hoogbouw`) and **subject vegetation** when gardens, charbagh avenues, or forested ridges belong to the monument itself (lobed tapering `cypress` in `taj-mahal`/`colosseum`/`hanging-gardens`, radial-frond `palm` and overhanging `vine` in `hanging-gardens`, tiered-cone `addPine` in `great-wall`, low `addSketchScrub` in `chichen-itza`, and parapet `hedgeRing` strips, using muted sage/olive washes `moss`, `leaf`, `pine`). Ground plants and tiered masses can throw flat shadow polygons offset by `-(lightDir.x / lightDir.y) * h, -(lightDir.z / lightDir.y) * h` in darkened `shade` wash (`shift: -0.10`, elevated `0.015–0.03` above the paving).
  - *Scale cues*: lofted scale visitors or carved relief figures (`addFigure` in `petra`) and tiny two-segment V-stroke birds (`bird(x, y, z, s)` in `hanging-gardens`) high above terraced monuments.
- **Site furniture** (every plate):
  - A closed ground slab / double-bordered plaza plinth (including side skirts and bottom face so low-angle orbits never reveal a paper-thin sheet), with courtyard paving grids (`fine`) clipped outside the monument's footprint.
  - A dashed survey grid beyond the plinth (`addDashedLineWorld`, `faint`), stopping `0.4–0.6` units short of the plinth walls rather than running through solid masonry.
  - Sketched shrubs/trees scattered by the PRNG outside the plinth, and ghost context masses at the plate corners in the `ghost` palette (`faint` ink). Because `ghost` matches the paper tone (`#e6e1d7` / `#e3ded4`), its solid faces cleanly occlude site grid lines behind it without competing with the hero palette — keep ghost masses at the periphery/corners, **never** centre-front where they block the subject.
  - Faint construction geometry where it earns its place: (1) 14–16 upper-left diagonal sky/projection rays fanning from a distant high point (e.g. `[-48, 50, -8]`) toward the monument, (2) a vertical dashed plumb/axis line rising through the apex or beside a leaning shaft (`pisa`, `chichen-itza`, `great-wall`, `colosseum`), and (3) proportion circles (`christ-the-redeemer`) or plan major/minor axes with extent ticks (`colosseum`).

## Modeling the subject

The engine guarantees the *style*; it guarantees nothing about the *architecture*. A plate can obey every rule above and still be a lesser building — that is exactly what happened with the first Taj Mahal plate (Nov 2026): correct paper, correct ink, correct seed, but a mausoleum suggested by stacked boxes where the real one is arches, bays, and a bulbous dome. The replacement — built from the decomposition below — is the standard. Style is the floor, not the result.

- **Decompose before you draw.** Write down the subject's real architectural components and give *each one its own builder*: for the Taj that list is pishtaq (the great framed arch), two-storey niche bays wrapping the chamfered corners, cylindrical drum, bulbous dome with lotus collar and finial, guldasta pinnacles, chhatris, minaret tiers with balcony rings. If a signature element comes out as a plain box with lines on it, the model is wrong — stop and write the builder.
- **Separate floor strata & layer depth contrast.** Never stack two wall boxes directly on top of each other without a projecting stringcourse or 3-part moulded cornice transition. Create positive/negative relief by backing open colonnades, arcades, and loggias with a darker inner core wall (`coreWall` / `stoneDark` / `void`), recessing window panels behind pilasters, and painting arch soffits and overhangs in deep sepia (`bot` / `dark.bot`) so bright foreground stonework pops in crisp relief.
- **Proportions come from the real building.** Take two or three actual dimensions and derive the ratios (dome diameter vs. plinth width, minaret height vs. dome height, bay width vs. total facade). Eyeballed massing drifts; ratio-derived massing reads as the building even before the details land.
- **Repeated elements are one builder, called in a loop.** Bays, niches, merlons, columns: parameterise once, place by the count you researched. Hand-placed near-copies are how counts and spacings go quietly wrong.
- **Frame the subject, not the site.** Fit `frustumSize` so the building fills the sheet; let gardens, plazas, and context crop at the edges. Shrinking the subject to fit its surroundings is how a monument becomes a diagram of its grounds (the first Taj ran frustum 78 and read small; the rebuild's 54 reads as a monument).
- **Real neighbours are buildings, not blocks.** Flanking structures that exist (the Taj's mosque and jawab) get the same treatment at lower ink weight — massing, a face, a roof silhouette. A blank ghost box is for structures that are *absent or unknown*, not for ones you didn't model.
- **Context is part of the deliverable.** Whoever builds a plate needs this skill, the exemplar plates (`christ-the-redeemer`, `taj-mahal`) open beside them, *and* the decomposition list for the subject. A builder working from the style contract alone will produce style-correct, architecture-wrong work — plan the brief accordingly.

## Workflow

1. **Research the subject before drawing it.** The plates are credible because the counts are right: 80 bays and three arcades on the Colosseum, eight tiers on Pisa, six columns on the Treasury facade, 91 steps per face on El Castillo. Get the real proportions, the tier/bay/column counts, and the one or two structural facts the drawing should teach (Pisa's corrective curve, the Colosseum's exposed hypogeum, the Taj's 1.4° outward-leaning minarets). Decide what the cutaway or opening shows. The output of this step is the decomposition list from *Modeling the subject*, not a vibe.
2. **Choose the seed year and the palettes.** Paper, ink, and light never change; the subject's materials do (rose sandstones for Petra, cream marble + terracotta for Pisa, white granite + grassy terraces for Machu Picchu). Add palettes, don't retune the engine.
3. **Start from `plates/template.html`.** Rename the title, set the seed, delete the demo rotunda, keep the engine and site furniture.
4. **Model in passes:** massing first (does the silhouette read?), then structure and courses, then the signature details, then the opening/cutaway, then furniture. Run it between passes.
5. **Fit the camera** (`frustumSize` or `fittedFrustum(aspect)`, `targetCenter`) last, once the massing is final.
6. **Verify** (below). Fix what the screenshots show, not what the code suggests.
7. **Deliver** the standalone file as `plates/<slug>.html` in this repo. The file is the deliverable — it must open and run by itself.

## Verification

Not optional, and not "the code looks right":

- Render screenshots at desktop 1280×800 and mobile 390×844 and actually look at them.
- Adversarial pass on each shot: Is the subject centred and fully in frame at both aspects? Does the ink hierarchy read (silhouette bold, detail quiet)? Do ghost masses and backdrop peaks stay out of the subject's face? Any z-fighting shimmer, missing faces, or hatching on the lit side? Do the researched counts match what's drawn? Does every signature element read as itself (an arch as an arch, a dome as a dome) rather than as a box suggesting it? Is the caption on its paper patch, clear of the drawing, at both aspects? Are real neighbouring buildings modelled, not blank blocks?
- Orbit it: drag, zoom, double-click reset. The drawing must hold from every azimuth and polar angle, not just the default diagonal — check cutaway interiors, back faces, and the ground-plate skirt.
- Geometry health check: scan `washPositions` for non-finite values (`!isFinite(washPositions[i])`) and optionally expose `window.__<slug> = { tris, washVerts, ink, badVerts }` (as in `colosseum.html` and `hanging-gardens.html`) so automated Playwright checks can assert `badVerts === 0` and verify triangle/segment counts across all ink layers.
- If the sandbox can't reach the CDNs, test with locally vendored copies of the same two scripts — but ship the CDN version.
- Confirm zero console errors and that the file's only external references are the two CDN scripts.
